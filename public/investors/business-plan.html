01Executive summary

Everest ALT is not an AI company. It is the infrastructure layer that lets an AI companion or assistant a person already talks to send a real gift into the physical world — on a budget that person controls, fulfilled by a network of local makers rather than a warehouse.

The thesis rests on two bodies of evidence that rarely share a room. First, decades of research show that spending money on other people — especially people we're close to — produces more happiness than spending it on ourselves. Second, a fast-growing literature on AI companionship finds real benefit alongside a real risk: that AI relationships can displace human ones rather than strengthen them.

Everest ALT is built at that intersection on purpose. Instead of another companion competing for attention, we give existing companions a way to point outward — toward real people — in the form of something that arrives at a door. The AI notices. The gift is real. The relationship strengthened is a human one.

02The problem — a widening connection gap

Two trends are converging, and most companies build for only one.

Loneliness is a public-health crisis.

In 2023 the U.S. Surgeon General declared loneliness and isolation an epidemic, citing health risk comparable to smoking up to fifteen cigarettes a day. Roughly half of U.S. adults report loneliness, and poor social connection is linked to a 29% higher risk of heart disease and elevated risk of early death.[4][5]

Social connection is a critical and underappreciated contributor to individual and population health, community safety, resilience, and prosperity.— Office of the U.S. Surgeon General, 2023 [4]

AI companionship is growing — and the research is not simply celebratory.

AI companions can reduce short-term loneliness and offer a low-stakes space to practice emotional expression. But peer-reviewed 2024–25 work also documents people reporting they feel closer to an AI than to their closest human friends, and warns of a displacement effect in which time and self-disclosure meant for people get redirected to an artificial relationship.[19][20][22][23]

The honest reading: AI companionship's effect depends on how it's designed. A companion built purely to maximize engagement risks becoming a closed loop. A companion that notices a person's real relationships and acts on their behalf toward those relationships has the opposite shape. Everest ALT is a bet on the second design.

03Our thesis — why this creates real impact

Giving makes people happier than spending on themselves — and closeness amplifies it.

A well-replicated body of research (Dunn, Aknin, Norton) finds prosocial spending produces greater happiness than spending the same money on oneself — observed across dozens of studies and many countries, suggesting a psychological near-universal.[1][2] Crucially, the effect is strongest under specific conditions: when the giver sees the impact, when it feels freely chosen, and above all when the gift goes to someone they're close to.[6] That is precisely the shape of every Everest ALT transaction.

A gift that arrives is different from a message that is read.

A text or a like is cheap to produce and cheap to ignore. A physical object at someone's door carries weight a screen can't replicate — it required a decision, a cost, and follow-through. Everest ALT's value proposition is converting a moment of AI-generated noticing into that kind of weighted, physical statement, instead of letting it evaporate as one more notification.

The part that matters most to a responsible investor

Most companion products are structurally exposed to the displacement risk, because their model depends on maximizing time on the AI. Everest ALT's model does not. We succeed only when a companion directs a person's attention and resources outward, toward a real spouse, sibling, or friend. Every successful transaction is, by definition, a moment where an AI relationship strengthened a human one. Our growth and our stated social impact point the same way.

04What we're building

Infrastructure, not personality.

We do not build AI companions and we are not trying to compete with the companies that do. We are the rail that lets any companion or assistant a person already uses connect to a real spending budget and a real fulfillment network of local gift partners. Our closest comparisons are Stripe and Twilio: infrastructure other products build on, rather than a product competing for the same attention.

  • The person sets a monthly budget. The AI never sees payment details — only the number.
  • The person tells their companion what to notice: a partner's birthday, a friend's hard week, a sibling's good news.
  • When the moment arrives, a gift is sent — drop-shipped from a partner near the recipient, not a central warehouse.
  • Every send is logged, reviewable, and capped by a limit only the person controls.

The 60-second connection.

The single biggest threat to adoption is setup friction. Our standard: a person goes from curiosity to a working, funded connection in under sixty seconds — three questions any adult already knows the answer to (who do you love, how much per month, which AI do you use), with no need to understand APIs, tokens, or how models call tools.

An honest note on what "connecting" means today

Not every platform exposes a way for a third party to be triggered by the AI itself. A small number support genuine tool use, where the companion calls an external service directly; most consumer companion apps do not yet. Where deep integration exists, the AI triggers a send directly; where it doesn't, Everest ALT's own system executes on the schedule and occasions the person defined. The person's experience — and the emotional outcome — is identical. Full technical detail is in How the system works.

05Go-to-market

The language that sells this is the same language that makes it true.

This is a category where overclaiming is easy and where the academic literature — and the emotional stakes for real people — punish it quickly. Three rules govern our messaging:

Lead with the relationship

Open with a person and a moment — a birthday, a hard week — not AI, budgets, or infrastructure. The technology is the explanation, never the headline.

Name the objection first

Answer the two silent questions up front: the AI only ever sees a budget number, never the card; and this is for anyone you love, not just "AI girlfriends."

Prove it with a number

60-second setup, a hard cap, a gift from a maker within a defined radius. Falsifiable specifics, not "seamless" and "magical."

Initial acquisition converts the existing waitlist, then compounds through a built-in referral mechanic (each referred signup advances a person's place in line) and organic word-of-mouth from a product whose entire output is a delightful surprise arriving at someone's door.

08Market opportunity

The intersection of three large, growing markets.

Everest ALT sits across the AI companion and personal-agent market, the U.S. gifting/floral/specialty-gift retail market, and the e-commerce logistics layer that connects online intent to local physical fulfillment.

Rather than assert a single blended TAM without primary research behind it, we present the opportunity as a thesis: as companion and assistant adoption grows, and as loneliness and connection concerns continue to draw public, regulatory, and consumer attention, demand for products that responsibly convert AI engagement into real human connection is likely to grow with it. A detailed, sourced market-sizing model is a natural next deliverable once funded.

10Risks & responsible design

We'd rather name these than have an investor or journalist name them for us.

Category reputational risk

AI companionship has produced real harm in documented cases. We're not a companion platform and generate no companion personality or emotional content — and our product only functions by pointing a person toward another real human, the opposite of the documented harms. Still, we inherit some scrutiny and plan for it.[21]

Integration depth varies today

Not every platform can trigger us automatically yet. This is a real, current limitation we represent accurately rather than imply uniform deep integration everywhere.

Trust & fraud in agent spend

Agent-initiated purchases — even under a hard cap — draw fraud attempts and demand spend controls, approval thresholds, and transaction monitoring from day one, not once volume grows.

Regulatory attention

Consumer protection and AI regulation are active, moving areas. A product touching AI companionship and payments should build compliance and legal review into the roadmap, not treat it as a later concern.
